About

Grounded in electrochemistry, aimed at the energy transition.

Daniela is an electrochemist and materials scientist, with a Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering from Stanford University and over a decade of research experience across energy systems, advanced materials, and semiconductor technology. At Stanford, she designed and operated bipolar-membrane water electrolyzers for hydrogen production from impure feedstocks, achieving an 8x reduction in chlorine oxidation and a 140x improvement in device lifetime versus a PEM baseline, and is first author on a paper in Joule. Earlier, at NREL, she synthesized biomass-derived composite materials; as an undergraduate, she did research on atmospheric aerosols and viscous fingering stabilization; and she has evaluated the technical and commercial feasibility of market entry for a hydrogen company in Scotland.

She has contributed to grant proposals supporting $600K+ in competitively awarded research funding, mentored and trained 100+ researchers on shared instrumentation, and now works as a R&D Consultant in materials science, developing analytical protocols for next-generation semiconductor lithography materials.
